# Decision experiments: results of October 16, 2026

These are the experiments of concept Section 16.7 (review Section D), run with the desktop harness ([`harness/`](../../harness/src/main/kotlin/ch/lkmc/chessandcocoa/harness/Main.kt)) on the code as committed. They play the real coach pipeline (the same `Coach` facade the app uses) against a **weakened-Stockfish learner proxy**: a shallow MultiPV search with a softmax over scores and an 8% chance of a random move. The proxy is not a model of human players; Maia-2 replies and real learners were not used.

Per-profile reports, 16 games each, at most 100 plies:

- [Gentle](2026-10-16-gentle.md)
- [Steady](2026-10-16-steady.md)
- [Keen](2026-10-16-keen.md)
- [Latency, uncontended](2026-10-16-latency.md)

The per-profile runs used 15 engines in parallel on 16 cores, so their E4 tables overstate latency; the latency run used one engine. All runs are reproducible: the same seeds give the same games (single-thread, node-limited searches with a cleared hash; seeded concessions).

## What changed during the experiments

The first runs (two families: weak king square and weak unit) gave a median of 1 plan event per game and 7 to 17% plan influence. Two changes followed, both kept:

1. **Applicability requires a sound step.** Rejected plans were mostly "too costly": the best plan action was a median 265 to 450 centipawns worse than the best move, because templates proposed moves that simply lost the moving piece. Plan actions must now pass a static exchange check, and a plan applies only when it has at least one such action.
2. **A third family, promoting a passed pawn**, as the E3 rule prescribes ("switch to more frequent families"). Its success is checked outside the template: a promoted piece with the material kept.

For Steady they raised plan influence from 11% to 19%, completed plan episodes from 2 (in 12 games) to 14 (in 16 games), and the median plan events per game from 1 to 2.

After code review, two probe rules changed and Gentle and Keen were run again (Steady's results are unchanged):

3. **A strong reply is a fixed 50 centipawns from the best reply**, at every difficulty. It was the difficulty's plan window, which made Gentle (80) treat more replies as strong than Keen (30), the reverse of the concept's guidance that weaker profiles check less (Section 8.3).
4. **A strong reply that ends the game refutes the plan**, as one that blocks it does.

Keen now rejects more plans (influence 17% to 12%, median plan events 1.5 to 1.0); Gentle accepts slightly more (10% to 12%) but its plan moves are less specific (76% to 65%).

## Reading the results

- **Plans are real when they act.** When a plan is chosen it usually introduces or decides the move (E1 roles), and the progress check shows the chosen moves are mostly specific to the plan. Policy identity held in every one of 286 re-run probe branches.
- **Plans act too rarely for the review's thresholds.** Most rejections are still "too costly" or "refuted in the probe": in ordinary positions the templates often have no move within the quality window that also survives the strongest replies. The opponent then plays a checked fallback and the coach stays with tactical feedback, which is the concept's intended abstention, but it means fewer plan explanations.
- **Around 40% of adopted episodes close at the next own decision** (37 to 46%). Against a random-ish proxy, many replies change the position enough to break a plan's dependencies; with real learners this rate is unknown.

## Next steps the rules point to

- Do not raise the probe horizon (E3 and E1 are not met).
- Grow coverage with further checkable families (open files, pawn breaks, endgame goals) and measure each one before keeping it.
- Run E4 on target phones, and E2/E3 with Maia-2 replies, before relying on these numbers.
- Plan the learner pilot (E5); none of these results says anything about learning.
